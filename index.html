How to play

Fill in every empty box. Each card is one part of the circuit, with its voltage (V), current (A), and resistance (Ω). Every puzzle can be solved by logic alone.

Ohm's law

V = I × R

Works on every card, and on any group of cards. Here, 12 ÷ 2 = 6 Ω.

Series: one path

The current is the same through each part. Voltages add: 4 + 8 = 12 V. Resistances add: 2 + 4 = 6 Ω.

Parallel: side by side

Every branch gets the same voltage. Currents add: 1 + 2 = 3 A. Resistance: 1 ÷ (1/6 + 1/3) = 2 Ω.

Resistor color code

The first two bands are digits and the third is the multiplier. Gold multiplies by 0.1 and silver by 0.01. The last, separate gold band is tolerance, so skip it. Yellow, violet, gold is 47 × 0.1 = 4.7 Ω.

Turn on Color-code resistances in Settings to practice reading them.

The battery card holds the whole circuit's totals: its voltage, total current, and total resistance.

Puzzles

Easy uses whole numbers, Medium one decimal place, and Hard two. Classic gives you the battery voltage and every resistance. Mixed gives you any mix of values. Meter puzzles start with too few clues, so you measure the fewest boxes you can and try to match par.

Every circuit has three numbers

V

Voltage, in volts, is the push. A battery supplies it, like a pump pushing water through a pipe.

A

Current, in amps, is how much electricity flows, like the amount of water moving through the pipe.

Ω

Resistance, in ohms, is how hard a part is to push through, like a narrow section of pipe.

The moving dashes are current: out of the battery, through the resistor, and back.

Ohm's law connects them

V = I × R

Know any two and you can find the third. Cover the one you want in the triangle: I = V ÷ R, and R = V ÷ I.

12 V across 6 Ω → 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A

More push means more current. More resistance means less.

Series: one path

  • There's only one way around, so the same current flows through every part: 2 A everywhere.
  • Each part uses up some of the voltage. The drops add up to the battery's: 4 V + 8 V = 12 V.
  • Resistances add: 2 Ω + 4 Ω = 6 Ω. Check it with Ohm's law: 12 V ÷ 6 Ω = 2 A.

Parallel: side by side

  • Each branch connects across the same two points, so each one gets the full 6 V.
  • The current splits between branches and adds back up: 1 A + 2 A = 3 A.
  • More paths make it easier to get through, so the total is less than any one branch: 1 ÷ (1/6 + 1/3) = 2 Ω.
  • Shortcut: two equal branches halve the resistance. Two 6 Ω resistors side by side act like 3 Ω.

Treat a group as one resistor

  • R2 and R3 are in parallel, so together they act like one 2 Ω resistor.
  • That group is in series with R1: 2 + 2 = 4 Ω total.
  • 12 V through 4 Ω makes 3 A. R1 uses 6 V, which leaves 6 V for the group to split into 1 A and 2 A.

Solving an Ohmy puzzle

Dark numbers are clues. You fill in the rest, one step at a time. Here's one order that works:

  1. R2 ∥ R3: 1 ÷ (1/6 + 1/3) = 2 Ω
  2. Total resistance: 2 + 2 = 4 Ω
  3. Total current: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A
  4. R1: 3 × 2 = 6 V, leaving 12 − 6 = 6 V
  5. R2: 6 ÷ 6 = 1 A. R3: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 A

No guessing needed. If you get stuck, look for a card or group where you already know two of the three numbers.
